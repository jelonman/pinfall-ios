# Saturation check, run 2026-08-01, before shipping anything

The standing rule on this box is that having an idea is the START of validation, never evidence
of anything, and that the first idea anyone would have is by definition the most competed one.
Pull-the-pin is the most cloned mobile mechanic there is. So this check was run against live
sources rather than assumed, and it is written down whether or not it is convenient.

## What the market actually says

**The mechanic is saturated.** The App Store carries the Popcore original plus a visible tail of
clones — *Pull The Pin 2026*, *Pull The Pin - Pin Pull Game*, *Pull the Pin - Pull Pin Games*,
*Pull the Pin: Puzzle Games*, *Pin Rescue* — from five different publishers, several of them
one-person operations. Popcore's own stated advantage is a release cadence that scales a viral
mechanic **before** category saturation. We are years past that point on this one.

**Pure hypercasual leaks players.** Hypercasual posts competitive Day 1 and then sheds almost
everyone by Day 30. Puzzle as a genre retains ~31.9% D1, ~12.2% D7, ~5.4% D30.

**Hybrid-casual is what is working in 2026.** D7 retention around 2.5x hypercasual and ARPDAU
4-7x higher; hybrid-puzzle titles hit ~16% D7 against hypercasual's single digits, with market
share up over 240% in high-intent markets.

**The winners buy their way in.** Leading studios ship ~123 creatives a month, 58% of them new,
purely to outrun ad fatigue. That is a user-acquisition machine, and this operation does not
have one.

## What this changes, and what it does not

It does not change the mechanic. The owner asked for the games in the ads, explicitly, and that
call is his. The pull is also genuinely good raw material: it reads in three seconds of footage,
which is the hardest property to retrofit.

What it changes is the SHAPE around the mechanic. Shipping a pure hypercasual pin-puller in 2026
means competing on the one axis we are worst at — paid acquisition volume — while accepting the
worst retention curve on the board. So the build carries a hybrid layer instead: progress that
persists between sessions, so a returning player has something waiting rather than starting from
nothing. That is the single difference the data attributes the 2.5x D7 gap to, and it is cheap
to add now and expensive to retrofit later.

## The honest expectation

This should not be planned around as a revenue line. Against a saturated mechanic with no UA
budget, the realistic outcome is a small organic trickle. Its actual value is different and
worth being clear about: it is the first thing in this portfolio that a stranger can judge on
sight, it exercises the entire iOS pipeline end to end, and a working pipeline is what makes the
NEXT game — on a less-competed mechanic — a week of work instead of a month.
